Basketball Strategy · GRAVITY Co., Ltd.

NBA RISE is an officially licensed NBA card game. You play as a team owner and head coach, never as a player on the court. You scout real players from card packs, train them, build a 13-man roster around synergies and tactics, then coach a full 48-minute 3D simulation of each game. This dossier covers what the game is, every system that has been documented, where it came from and how it is performing. Spec sheet

What it is

Two games carry this name

NBA RISE (global, 2026) is the game on the store page you shared. GRAVITY's Seoul headquarters publishes it and built it in-house. It launched on 20 October 2026 in eight Asia-Pacific markets. It comes from a Japanese predecessor, NBA RISE TO STARDOM, which ran from 2021 to 2024, was relaunched as NBA RISE in 2025, and ended on 31 August 2026. That version was run by GRAVITY's Tokyo subsidiary, Gravity Game Arise. The two use separate apps, accounts, logos and operators. This dossier covers both, and labels which version each fact belongs to.

The game in one paragraph

You run an NBA franchise built from collectible player cards and never touch the ball. You open packs, level and promote cards, and field five starters plus an eight-man bench chosen for position fit and synergy bonuses. You set four offensive and defensive tactics against the opponent's plan. Then you coach a full 48-minute game rendered as a 3D sports broadcast. During the game you make substitutions, swap tactics, and once per quarter play a SET PLAY, a timing tap that swings team morale. Wins feed a league ladder that runs from “Rookie 3” to the NBA Finals, plus PvP leaderboards and club-versus-club wars. The tagline is “Collect. Build. Rise.”

Narrative

Story & framing

There is no story campaign and no cast of original characters. The game's fiction is a role: you are the owner and head coach of an NBA team. The developer calls Korean players 구단주님 (“Team Owner”) and English players “Riser”. The store copy reads: “Coach from the sideline as each game plays out as a simulation.” The beta site read: “From rookie to legend, start your professional journey in the NBA.”

  • The arc is the ladder. Season Mode starts you in the “Rookie 3” league and promotes you tier by tier toward an NBA Finals series.
  • The real NBA calendar sets the content schedule. Cards follow current rosters (2025-26 at launch). Themed packs follow the season (a Halloween edition ran 29 Oct–12 Nov), and the Season Pass changes monthly.
  • The marketing carries the narrative. GRAVITY released an animated series, “The Dream” (Episode 1 has 579K English views on the official YouTube channel), plus an animation teaser with 1.9M views.
  • The predecessor told real NBA stories. The Japanese version ran “History Events” that unlocked story chapters as you levelled a featured player. Examples include “EARNED, NOT GIVEN”, the Yuta Watanabe story, and “BULLS DYNASTY”. That version's tutorial was hosted by Yuta Watanabe. The global version has none of these.

The global game keeps the franchise's core design: auto-simulated matches broken up by player-triggered set plays, scouting, training and promotion, a six-tier rarity ladder ending at LEGEND, and a tower event (now “Tower Race”). How it plays

Core loop

  1. CollectScout packs, free daily packs (10 cards a day from login), quest rewards, the Auction and coupon codes.
  2. DevelopTraining (levels), Promotion, Grade Enhancement, Training Manuals, EXP cards and Club Facilities.
  3. Build5 starters + 8 bench. Stack synergies, assign 4 tactic slots and field a head coach.
  4. PlayA 48-minute 3D simulation. Substitutions, tactic swaps, one SET PLAY per quarter, or Auto and Skip.
  5. CompeteSeason ladder, Arena, Popularity Battle, Tower Race, Daily Division and Club Conquest.

↻ Competition pays Gold, Silver and packs, which go back into Collect.

Simulation

Match engine

Every game is simulated on a real NBA clock. The match screen opens at Q1 12:00 and runs a 24-second shot clock, which means four 12-minute quarters of game time. The engine resolves play from player ratings, condition, stamina, synergies and the tactic matchup. You watch it as a TV-style broadcast with 3D player models on a full arena court with crowd. Quarter, team power, points and rebounds update live.

What's on screen

  • Performance Comparison. Paired bars for ALL, PG, SG, SF, PF and C show who is winning each positional matchup.
  • Team Morale. A morale bar that grants a stat buff badge (e.g. “+5% stats”) when you are ahead on it.
  • Live player cards show points and assists, with event pop-ups such as “REBOUND”.
  • Stamina and condition. Each card has a stamina bar and a condition arrow (green up, yellow flat, orange down-right, red down). Condition changes a player's stats.
  • Controls: Details · Substitution · Tactic Change · AUTO PLAY · SKIP · Pause · QUIT, plus an AUTO SUBSTITUTION toggle and automatic tactic selection.
  • An adaptive AI coach. Mid-game the opponent switches plans, announced as “The opposing team has changed its tactics” with the rival coach's portrait.

SET PLAY: the one hands-on moment

Once per quarter the game stages a set play around a set mission: Shoot, Block, Drive or Layup. A 3-2-1 countdown starts, the camera cuts to a 3D one-on-one with both players' cards on screen, and a ring closes around an action button (a 1.9-second timer is shown in the screenshots). Tap inside the window to complete the play.

  • Success raises team morale by 30%. That is the biggest single swing you control.
  • A tired player has a narrower window. The lower the stamina, the tighter the timing, so stamina management feeds directly into your execution.
  • Commentary lines play over the cutscene (“Unbelievable individual skill! The defense has completely collapsed!”). The cutscene cannot be skipped, and players have asked for that option.

Rotation is a real decision

Stamina drains on court and recovers on the bench. Korean outlet iNews24's hands-on test concluded that “you have to run a rotation using the bench rather than relying only on starters”. The same review compared mid-game counter-tactics to Football Manager. If you would rather not manage this, Full Auto Mode, Auto Substitution and SKIP handle it. SKIP is padlocked on early match screens and unlocks with progression. One player reported different results from skipping at tip-off and skipping mid-game. There are no timeouts. “Clutch” appears only as a player stat.

Strategy layer

Tactics

Tactics are chips placed in four slots, and you can split them between offense and defense any way you like (screenshots show 1+3, 2+2 and 3+1). There are five offensive priorities and five defensive schemes, and each defensive scheme mirrors one offensive priority. The tactic list has a 상성 (“matchup”) column. It marks each option ▲ (advantage) or ▼ (disadvantage) against what the opponent is running at that moment.

How a tactic is tuned

  • Offensive chips target a position and carry a percentage, e.g. “PG Drive Priority 60%” or “SG Drive Priority 73%”. That focuses the possession on one player's role.
  • Defensive chips have three intensities: Low (하), Mid and Strong (강). Strong costs more stamina; the list's “stamina consumption” column shows two ⚡ for Strong against one for the others.
  • Each player's card lists the chips assigned to them and a ⚡ load from 1 to 6. The more tactics you pile on one player, the faster that player tires.
  • A head coach card sits beside your tactics. Seen so far: Erik Spoelstra, JJ Redick, Brian Keefe and Mark Daigneault. Coaches have their own owned-collection screen. They appear in no published pack table, and GRAVITY has not published what each coach does.

The Korean store pitch sums up the design intent: “선수 별 포지션 배치와 상성에 맞는 뇌지컬 전술이 승리의 치트키!” (“Placing each player by position and using brainy tactics that fit the matchups is the cheat code to victory!”)

Anatomy of a card

  • OVR, the overall rating, in the top-left corner
  • Position, single or dual (PG/SG, SF/SG, PF/C)
  • Team logo, level, edition label (“THE Core”, “25-26 SEASON”), rarity badge
  • In formation: condition arrow, stamina bar and an “S n” badge counting the synergies the player contributes to
  • OVR grows steeply with level. Lv 1 Commons sit at 61–120. Lv 1 Ultra Rares sit at 123–145. Lv 30 Epics reach 228–335.
  • A Lv 1 Ultra Rare can outrate a low-level Epic, so levelling matters as much as rarity.
  • Named attributes so far: Chance Creation (기회 창출), Defensive Pressure (수비 압박) and Clutch (클러치).

Growth, duplicates and disposal

  • Training raises a card's level. It costs Silver and uses EXP cards and Intermediate or Advanced Training Manuals. Silver is the main bottleneck.
  • Promotion and Grade Enhancement (등급 강화) raise a card's tier and its ceiling. Launch events paid out for completing five enhancements.
  • Duplicates don't fuse. Spare cards go to the Exchange, where 3 players buy one item, or you Sell them for Silver. The Exchange stocks Epic-or-better packs for each edition, a Legend-or-better pack for The Edition, plus Small Energy Drinks, Small First-Aid Kits and Condition Boosters. When players asked for duplicate-fusion skill upgrades, the developer said it would pass the idea to the team.

Roster construction

Team building

A roster is 5 starters plus an 8-man bench, 13 players in all. The bench header counts positions (e.g. “PG 3 · SG 1 · SF 1 · PF 2 · C 1”) so you can see gaps in your rotation. The team is summarised in four numbers: Team Power (TP) with its synergy bonus shown separately, Offense, Defense and Team Morale. On the match-prep screen in the store art, the team shows TP 11,717 with a +2,184 bonus from 10 active synergies.

2021 – 2026

The Japanese predecessor

Gravity Game Arise's official news archive (480 posts on nbarise.com) documents the original design in full. It shows where the global game's systems came from.

NBA RISE TO STARDOM (2021–24)

  • Roster: head coach + 5 starters + 8 bench + 2 reserves. You start by choosing one of the 30 NBA teams.
  • Six stats per card: SHOOT, DRIVE and PASS on offense; STEAL, BLOCK and PASSCUT on defense; plus Stamina. Playing a card at its favoured position gives a stat bonus.
  • Simulation: possessions resolve as player-vs-player stat duels. You watch “Live View” highlights or a “Card View” card battle for every play.
  • Clutch Play: when the Clutch Gauge fills, you pick a player and an action. Shoot, pass and drive face block, steal and pass-cut in a rock-paper-scissors cycle: the right counter multiplies your value ×1.5, but raw stats still win first. The winner gains Momentum, and the side with higher Momentum wins tied duels.
  • Stamina: at zero a player's abilities are halved. Bench players recover faster.
  • Head-coach tactics: a stat modifier (e.g. Drive +1 / Block −1), a key position, and a stamina cost. Coaches cost 30,000 Money each.
  • Gacha (“Offer”): 5-card packs and 150-card BOXes. Three copies of a card raise its rarity (R → UR). Skill Training takes 24 hours and needs an Epic trainer.
  • Modes: an 82-game season (PLAY-MATCH or SIM-MATCH), Score Ranking (async PvP), real-time Player Match on a shared card pool, and History and VS events.
  • Trade: billed as an “industry-first online trade”. NPC Legend listings at 4,500 Gold refresh every 3 days.
  • Base: an isometric city with 9 facility types, fans and an Official Store that generates Money.

NBA RISE, Japan (2025–26)

  • Pitch: “Games basically run on auto. At key moments you can trigger a Set Play in which you choose players' actions… succeed and Tension rises.”
  • Simulation view: a 2D top-down court with position tokens, tactic labels (e.g. “Isolation Lv3”), a tug-of-war bar, AUTO and “Skip quarter”.
  • Set Play: a 3D possession of up to 4 actions (pass, pass, then shoot or drive) with a “KEY PLAYER”. Defensive set plays exist too.
  • Super Play: the signature skill on Epic and Legend cards (“Tatum Sky”, “Dame Rain”, Hachimura's “Rising Fuji”), e.g. Shoot +45 for −125 stamina.
  • Tension + Tension Links: Tension replaces Momentum. Tension Links (Offense/Defense/Stamina Starter and Plan) are conditional team bonuses.
  • Stats rescaled to three digits: e.g. Lv 70 Epic Jokić has Shoot 261, Pass 231, Drive 229, Stamina 1,566. Ultra Rares cap at Lv 60, Epics and Legends at Lv 70.
  • Action Points (cap 100) gate Season play, and a calendar month can be auto-simulated.
  • Scouts: standard, event-boost, limited, and START DASH (an Epic after 5 purchases). Standard rates: EPIC 1.5%, UR 10%, R 20%, UC 30%, C 38.5%.
  • Events: VS, History (raise a player's rarity with points) and Tower (beat the team on each floor).
  • Ambassadors: Yuki Kawamura and Rui Hachimura. Watanabe served earlier.

What carried into the global game

  • The rarity ladder C · UC · R · UR · EPIC · LEGEND carried over unchanged.
  • The Set Play hook survived. Japan used a multi-action choice chain; the global game condenses it into a once-per-quarter timing tap.
  • The swing meter changed from Momentum to Tension to Team Morale.
  • Matchup logic moved from the clutch duel into the tactic layer. Japan's shoot/drive/pass vs block/steal/pass-cut triangle became five offensive priorities, each mirrored by a defensive scheme.
  • Tower events became Tower Race, and the “Scout” name and the Training and Promotion steps were kept.
  • Global is noticeably more generous on pity (Epic guaranteed at 100 cards), but its base Epic rate is lower (0.5% vs Japan's 1.5% standard scout).

What players complain about

  • Access and stability, the biggest negative cluster. Players report being stuck on the loading or title screen, often because they are outside the eight regions. Others report a black screen after the NBA logos, “not compatible” errors after 1.1.1, top-ups charged but not delivered (Thailand), and broken Tower Race, Club and Match Prediction rewards.
  • Gacha and pay-to-win: “50 pulls no good card”, “Good mechanics but rates are low” (17 helpful votes), and “P2W game, period.”
  • Pacing and fatigue: “The games take way too long with animation that is totally unnecessary… you have to manually subs tired players.” Other players say stamina drains faster for them than for the AI. A Thai reviewer misses the direct control of NBA Live Mobile, which had just shut down.
  • Depth: “Play ends up being just a few league games, finished with a few monotonous clicks.” Requests include bulk pack opening, legends such as Yao Ming and Kareem, and limiting PvP tactic changes to once per quarter.
  • Freshness and art: rosters are still 2025-26 although the 2026-27 season has started, and some reviewers accuse the art of being AI-generated.

Japan's arc

  • 2021–22: about 1.8★. Players cited a 0.2% top-rarity rate, launch crashes, lost data, unskippable 3D and “browser-game” quality.
  • 2023–24: stagnation. Reviews asked “Has it shut down?”
  • 2025 renewal: about 4.5★. Players welcomed auto mode and skip, though some complained that event leaderboards required event-gacha boost players.
  • 2026: abandonment. “No announcements since Jan 29… abandoned. I feel betrayed again.”
  • Final count: the App Store finished at 3.4★ from 1,858 ratings, with about 52.7K lifetime Android installs.

Corporate

Business

  • License terms. The May 2020 NBA + NBPA agreement covers “the right to develop and publish an NBA licensed game”, paid as license fees and guaranteed minimum royalties in installments. In 2025 it “was extended and additional license fees were committed.” Fee amounts are folded into aggregate purchase obligations (FY2025: ₩2.19bn due within 12 months).
  • GRAVITY's scale. FY2025 revenue was about ₩560bn, with about ₩77bn operating profit. Q2 2026 revenue was ₩161.9bn, of which mobile was ₩128.6bn. Ragnarok carries the company, running in 91 markets.
  • NBA RISE revenue has never been disclosed. The global launch was absent from GRAVITY's Q1 and Q2 2026 business updates. Q3 2026 results are due in mid-November.
  • Gravity Game Arise, the Japanese operator, had 60 staff in 2025. It lost ₩6.2bn on ₩5.6bn revenue in 2024 and ₩2.5bn on ₩15.0bn in 2025. That covers all of its titles.
  • A Korea launch was promised for the first half of 2022. It slipped to “2H 2022”, then “TBD”, and finally arrived on 20 Oct 2026 as part of the global release.
  • Infrastructure. The terms, privacy and support pages are hosted on withhive.com, Com2uS's Hive platform.
  • GRAVITY's other licensed sports titles: CPBL Pro-baseball 2021 (Taiwan) and JLPGA Heroine Collection (Japan, 2025). Both use the same licensed-sports card model.
