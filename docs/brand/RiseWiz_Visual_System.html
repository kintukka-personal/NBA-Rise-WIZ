Visual system · NBA RISE companion

Their colour.
Our marker.

The visual system for RiseWiz, the companion to GRAVITY's NBA RISE. Everything that shows the game's data is measured, from a corpus of 424 screenshots of the global build. 142 of them show full in-game UI, across 42 distinct screens. That includes the typeface, which was identified by glyph overlap. NBA RISE paints with every hue on the wheel, so RiseWiz takes no colour of its own. It takes a material instead: black marker on a white coach's clipboard. Nothing in the game looks like that.

Identity

The third sibling

RISEWIZ

The site is RiseWiz. The name splits in two, and so does the design:

  • RISE is the game's world. Anything that shows the game's own data is drawn in the measured system in Foundation through Rarities. That covers cards, OVR, rarities, condition, tactics, packs and the market.
  • WIZ is ours. Our analysis, our play diagrams and our teaching material go on the clipboard.

One rule settles which: their data goes out on the broadcast, our thinking goes on the clipboard.

Why RISE and not the acronym

FHCWiz and UFMWiz both take the game's acronym. Here the acronym would start with NBA, which is the league's trademark, not the game's. So the family pattern takes the game's own word: RISE. It is the part of the title GRAVITY chose, and it carries no league mark.

The mark

A W run off two screens. The shooter starts at the circle and cuts to the baseline. They curl off the first screen, drift back up the lane, then come off the second screen to the wing and catch. In basketball's own notation, the bar at each lower point of the W is a screen and the filled disc is the ball arriving. It is drawn in black marker on a white clipboard with the half-court printed faintly underneath.

It is the one piece of the identity that owes nothing to the game, and it survives intact if the word ever has to go.

The wordmark

RISEWIZ in Big Shoulders Display Black, one line, with a rule beneath the full width. RISE sits in #8B94A2, the game's own inactive-nav grey, and WIZ sits in #FFFFFF. The acronym names the subject and the brand is the lit half.

The rule is a marker stroke in board white, #F5F5F1, with round ends. The game never draws a round-capped white stroke on slate, so the lockup reads as ours without borrowing a colour.

  • Never set RISE alone, in any face, at any size.
  • Never set RISEWIZ in one flat colour. The halves always differ in value.
  • Never put "NBA" in the wordmark, beside it or above it. The league's name is never brand type.
  • Never colour the rule. It is the marker, and marker on slate is white.

FHCWiz is chalk on a dark board and UFMWiz is marker on grass. RiseWiz is the only light surface in the family. The three can sit side by side and never be mistaken for each other.

Foundation

The black is cool

FHC's black is chromatically dead and UFM's leans warm. NBA RISE's leans blue. Across the full Match Preparation screen, 89% of dark pixels carry more blue than red, with a mean R−B of −9.7 over 1.88 million samples. #2A2E32 alone covers 31% of the screen.

Elevation, the RISE way

Nothing is glossed. The Q1 START button is #00AEEF from its top pixel row to its bottom one. MENU is #3D454B top to bottom. Tactic chips, pack tiles and bars are flat too. A surface reads as raised in two ways:

  • A step in value. Panels sit one or two ramp steps above or below the ground. Recessed wells, like the OVR box and the nameplate, are darker than the ground.
  • A glow. The primary button carries a cyan glow beneath it, and a selected comparison row lights a cyan ring. The PLAY ball glows orange. Glow is the only elevation the game uses, and it always comes in the element's own colour.

The one light place. The home hub sits on a sky gradient, #093750 → #388AA9 → #B7C9CF, with white HUD pills (#EBF0F4). It is the only light ground in the game. Every working screen is slate.

Every hue is spoken for

FHCWiz reuses the game's red for its pen, and UFMWiz found a bib orange the game never uses. We looked for the RiseWiz equivalent and there isn't one.

We searched the full colour space for the colour farthest from all 34 measured accents. Each candidate was scored under normal vision and simulated protan, deutan and tritan vision. The best possible result is only 6.3 ΔE from its nearest game colour, which is below the 8 ΔE a categorical colour needs to stand alone. Once the colour must also read on the white board and on the slate, the winner is a purple or a blue: both are the game's.

So the brand is black and white. The game never draws dark strokes on a white surface, and the clipboard owns that.

Type

Open Sans, identified, not guessed

FHC's typeface was never identified. NBA RISE's was. The in-game UI is set in Open Sans, at Bold for interface text and ExtraBold for screen titles. It is rendered through TextMeshPro with a thin dark outline.

We tested 32 faces against crops of "MATCH PREPARATION" and "Warriors Teamwork" at matched height. The candidates were Open Sans, Noto Sans and Noto Sans KR, Pretendard, Nexon Football Gothic, S-Core Dream, Inter, Source Sans, Fira, Roboto, Hind, Mukta and others. On mixed case, Open Sans Bold matches the game's width-to-height ratio to the second decimal (12.80 against 12.81) and has the best glyph overlap (IoU 0.808).

Hind 700 scores slightly higher on the outlined caps title, because the outline thickens every stroke. On mixed case, though, Hind runs 7% narrower than the game, and no renderer narrows glyphs that much. The full score table ships in measurements/font_fit.json.

What the official site uses, and why we don't

GRAVITY's website (nbarise.gnjoy.com) loads Nexon Football Gothic, Pretendard, S-Core Dream, Oswald and Inter. None of them is the in-game face. They mark the marketing site, not the game. The store captions use a heavy condensed italic with a blue-to-red fill ("COLLECT YOUR STARS"); that is marketing art too.

RiseWiz's own face is Big Shoulders Display Black, used in the wordmark and nowhere else. FHC took Anton and UFM took Bebas Neue. Big Shoulders was drawn for Chicago, a basketball city, and appears nowhere in the game or on its site.

Texture

Almost none, on purpose

FHC carries its brand in torn paper and hazard stripes, and UFM in gold on black. NBA RISE carries it in absence: flat slate, flat fills, and a broadcast's restraint. Four layers, all CSS or inline SVG:

Clipboard

The WIZ half, the only thing here not measured

Everything above exists so the app can show the game's data faithfully. The clipboard exists so that when we explain something, it is obvious who is talking.

Dark and light

The game's world is dark and cool: slate, cyan, glow. The clipboard is light: a white dry-erase board in a black frame, with black marker on it. It is the only light working surface on any RiseWiz screen, and the eye finds it before reading a word.

Basketball's notation is the reverse of football's. UFMWiz's board draws the pass solid and the run dashed. A basketball clipboard draws the cut solid and the pass dashed. The two boards cannot be confused even in black and white.

The vocabulary — seven marks

This is how basketball coaches already draw, so it reads to anyone who has sat through a timeout. Adding an eighth mark means adding it here first.

One ball per figure. The filled disc marks where the possession finishes. It is the conclusion, so a figure with two balls is saying two things and should be two figures. It is the only filled mark on the board.

What the clipboard refuses

  • Never on a card. Cards, chips, frames and badges are the game's objects.
  • Never a number the game owns. No OVR, price, TP or odds on the board. The broadcast reports; the clipboard explains.
  • Never a game colour. No cyan, no team blue or red, no gold, nothing metal. Black and white only.
  • One ball per figure. A second ball is a second idea, so draw a second figure.
  • Never chrome. No clipboard-styled nav, buttons, headers or rules.
  • One board per screen. A second board competes with the first and neither reads.

Construction

Inline SVG on a viewBox 100 units wide (300 for figures), with height set by the drawing. No image files, no icon font, no drawing library. The same file draws the 16px favicon and a full-width figure.

Rules

Six decisions that settle most questions

Flat, never glossedOne colour per surface. The only gradients are the ball and the rarity metals, because the game's only gradients are the ball and the rarity metals.
Glow is elevationRaise with a glow in the element's own hue, never with a drop shadow. Recess with a darker well.
Home is blue, away is red, alwaysEvery two-sided view puts you in #158EFD on the left and them in #DF263D on the right. Never swap sides or colours, even in our own comparisons.
Cyan commitsCyan fills the action that moves you forward and the tab you are on. Cyan is never decoration.
Ahead is ice, behind is redWhen two numbers face off, the leader's turns #CAFFFF and the trailer's turns #E02540. That is the game's own matchup rule.
The broadcast reports, the clipboard explainsA surface showing the game's own numbers takes the measured system. A surface carrying our analysis takes the clipboard. Never mix them on one object: a whiteboard with an OVR on it is a fake game asset.

How it was measured

  • Corpus. 424 screenshots covering 42 distinct screens:
    • App Store sets in English and Korean: iPhone 1260×2736 and iPad 2048×2732.
    • Google Play sets in English, Korean, Thai, Indonesian and Filipino.
    • Korean hands-on press.
    • Player blogs: lossless 1080×1920 and 1399×3000 captures.
    • YouTube frames.
    142 images show full in-game UI. 51 of those come from the July 2026 closed beta and were used only where the launch build agrees. Colour was sampled from lossless PNGs wherever one existed. The catalogue lists every file with its page and image URL.
  • Colour. Median colour per component region plus four-colour median-cut clustering. Vertical profiles prove the fills are flat. Hue occupancy is computed across the corpus. The absent-colour search tests 3,000+ candidates in OKLab under normal vision and Machado protan, deutan and tritan simulation.
  • Type. 32 candidate faces rendered at matched cap height, scored by glyph IoU and aspect ratio against in-game crops.
  • Re-measuring. The scripts (sample.py, palette.py, huegap.py, absent.py) live in docs/brand/tools/. When a new rarity or screen ships, add its screenshot to the catalogue, sample it and add the token here first.

Limits

What we deliberately don't take

RiseWiz is a fan companion tool, not a GRAVITY product, and it has no licence from the NBA or the NBPA. The visual language of the game's interface is fair to mirror. Identity, marks and likenesses are not.

  • "NBA" is never brand type. It never appears in the wordmark, a logo, a headline treatment or a favicon. "NBA RISE" is written out only in prose to name the game, as the disclaimer does.
  • No league or team marks. No NBA logo or silhouette, no team logos or wordmarks, and no team colour schemes as decoration. Teams appear as three-letter tricodes in plain type.
  • No photography or renders. No player photos, card art, 3D models, arenas or store art. Cards are drawn in code with a plain silhouette.
  • Names and numbers as reference data only. Player names, ratings, odds and prices appear as facts the game publishes, never as endorsements.
  • No official framing. No GRAVITY marks, and no copy that implies partnership, endorsement or access to non-public data.
  • RISE only inside our wordmark. Never set alone, never shaped like the game's logo, never in the game's italic.
